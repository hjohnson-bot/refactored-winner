## 1. Checklist of All Possible Deductions

### Federal Deductions — Standard vs. Itemized

**Standard Deduction (2025 MFJ): ~$30,000**
Take the higher of standard or itemized. With W-2 + 1099 income in Indiana with 3 kids, review both.

#### Itemized Deductions (Schedule A)

- [ ] **State and local taxes (SALT)** — capped at $10,000
  - Indiana state income tax paid
  - Property taxes on your home
  - Either state income tax OR sales tax (whichever is higher)
- [ ] **Mortgage interest** — on up to $750K of acquisition debt
- [ ] **Charitable contributions** — cash (up to 60% AGI) and non-cash donations
  - Church tithing / donations
  - Goodwill / Salvation Army donations (get receipts with item values)
  - Mileage driven for charity (14 cents/mile)
- [ ] **Medical expenses** — only amounts exceeding 7.5% of AGI
  - Insurance premiums (if not pre-tax)
  - Copays, prescriptions, dental, vision
  - Mileage to medical appointments (22 cents/mile)
  - Long-term care premiums (age-based limits)

#### Above-the-Line Deductions (Available Even with Standard Deduction)

- [ ] **Self-employment tax deduction** — 50% of SE tax (automatic on Schedule SE)
- [ ] **Self-employed health insurance deduction** — if not eligible for spouse's employer plan
- [ ] **SEP-IRA / Solo 401(k) contributions** — up to $69,000 (2025)
- [ ] **Traditional IRA contributions** — $7,000 per person ($14,000 couple), income limits apply if covered by employer plan
- [ ] **HSA contributions** — $8,550 family (2025) if on a qualifying HDHP
- [ ] **Student loan interest** — up to $2,500 (income phase-outs apply)
- [ ] **Educator expenses** — $300 per qualifying teacher

#### Self-Employment / 1099 Deductions (Schedule C)

- [ ] **Home office deduction** — dedicated space used regularly and exclusively for business
  - Simplified method: $5/sq ft, up to 300 sq ft ($1,500 max)
  - Regular method: percentage of home expenses (mortgage interest, insurance, utilities, repairs)
- [ ] **Business vehicle expenses** — standard mileage (67 cents/mile for 2025) or actual expenses
  - Keep a mileage log (date, destination, business purpose, miles)
- [ ] **Business insurance** — liability, E&O, professional insurance
- [ ] **Professional development** — courses, certifications, books, conferences
- [ ] **Software and subscriptions** — tools used for business
- [ ] **Office supplies and equipment** — Section 179 or bonus depreciation for large purchases
- [ ] **Phone and internet** — business-use percentage
- [ ] **Professional services** — accounting, legal, consulting fees
- [ ] **Marketing and advertising** — website, business cards, ads
- [ ] **Business travel** — flights, hotels, 50% of meals while traveling
- [ ] **Business meals** — 50% deductible (must have business purpose documented)
- [ ] **Retirement plan contributions** — SEP-IRA, Solo 401(k), SIMPLE IRA
- [ ] **Bank fees and business credit card interest**

#### Tax Credits (Dollar-for-Dollar Reductions)

- [ ] **Child Tax Credit** — $2,000 per child under 17 (3 children = up to $6,000)
  - Phase-out starts at $400,000 AGI for MFJ
- [ ] **Child and Dependent Care Credit** — if paying for childcare so both spouses can work
  - Up to $3,000 (1 child) or $6,000 (2+ children) of expenses
  - Credit is 20-35% of expenses depending on income
- [ ] **Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC)** — income limits apply, 3+ children max ~$7,830
  - MFJ with 3 children: AGI must be under ~$63,398 (2025)
- [ ] **American Opportunity Tax Credit** — $2,500 per student (first 4 years of college)
- [ ] **Lifetime Learning Credit** — up to $2,000 per return
- [ ] **Saver's Credit** — for retirement contributions, income limits apply
- [ ] **Residential Energy Credits** — solar panels, heat pumps, insulation, windows
  - 30% of cost for solar (no cap)
  - Up to $3,200/year for energy efficiency improvements
- [ ] **Electric Vehicle Credit** — up to $7,500 for qualifying new EVs

## 3. Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Filing Mistakes
1. **Not tracking 1099 expenses** — The IRS sees your gross 1099 income; without deductions you pay tax on the full amount
2. **Missing quarterly estimated payments** — Leads to underpayment penalties (see Section 7)
3. **Forgetting self-employment tax** — 1099 income owes 15.3% SE tax on top of income tax
4. **Not maximizing retirement contributions** — This is the single biggest lever for reducing taxable income
5. **Overlooking the QBI deduction** — 20% deduction on qualified business income (Section 199A), available to most 1099 earners under income thresholds

### Indiana-Specific Mistakes
6. **Forgetting county tax** — Indiana has county income taxes on top of the state rate
7. **Not claiming Indiana credits** — See Section 8
8. **Paying Indiana estimated taxes late** — Separate from federal estimates

### General Mistakes
9. **Using "Married Filing Separately" without analysis** — MFJ is almost always better; MFS only helps in specific situations (income-driven student loan repayments, liability separation)
10. **Not keeping receipts** — If audited, no receipt = no deduction
11. **Mixing personal and business expenses** — Use a separate bank account and credit card for 1099 work
12. **Ignoring carryforward items** — Capital losses, NOLs, and excess charitable contributions carry forward

## 5. Tax-Saving Strategies You Can Still Implement

### Immediate Actions (Before Filing)

1. **Max out retirement contributions**
   - Traditional IRA: $7,000 each ($14,000 total) — deadline is April 15, 2026 for 2025 contributions
   - SEP-IRA: Up to 25% of net SE income — deadline is filing deadline (with extensions, Oct 15)
   - Solo 401(k): Employee contributions deadline was Dec 31, 2025; employer contributions due by filing deadline

2. **HSA contributions** — Can contribute up to April 15, 2026 for the 2025 tax year ($8,550 family)

3. **Review business expenses** — Go through bank/credit card statements to find missed deductions

4. **Harvest capital losses** — If you have investment losses, sell to offset gains (wash sale rules apply)

### Strategies for Next Year (2026)

5. **Set up a Solo 401(k)** — Must be established by Dec 31 of the tax year (unlike SEP-IRA)
   - Allows up to $23,500 employee + 25% employer = up to $69,000 total

6. **Income timing** — If 1099, you may be able to delay invoicing in December or accelerate expenses

7. **Bunch deductions** — If close to standard deduction threshold, bunch charitable giving and medical expenses into alternating years

8. **529 Plan contributions** — Indiana gives a 20% state tax credit on contributions up to $7,500 per taxpayer (max $1,500 credit per person, $3,000 per couple)
   - This is FREE money — contribute even if children are young

9. **Consider S-Corp election** — If 1099 income exceeds ~$50K net, an S-Corp can save significant SE tax by splitting income into salary (subject to SE tax) and distributions (not subject to SE tax)
   - Example: $100K net income, pay yourself $60K salary, take $40K distribution
   - SE tax savings: ~$6,120/year
   - Trade-off: Payroll costs ($500-2,000/year), extra compliance

10. **Maximize employer benefits** — 401(k) match, FSA/HSA, dependent care FSA ($5,000)

## 6. CPA vs. Tax Software

### You Should Use a CPA If:

| Factor | Your Situation | Verdict |
|--------|---------------|---------|
| Mixed W-2 and 1099 income | Yes | **CPA recommended** |
| Self-employment deductions | Yes (Schedule C) | **CPA recommended** |
| Multiple income sources | Yes | **CPA recommended** |
| Potential S-Corp election | Possibly | **CPA strongly recommended** |
| QBI deduction complexity | Yes | CPA helpful |
| State + federal filing | Yes | Software can handle |
| 3 dependents | Yes | Software can handle |

### Recommendation: **Use a CPA, at least for the first year**

**Why:**
- A good CPA will find deductions you'll miss — especially on the 1099 side
- SE tax planning and S-Corp analysis alone can save thousands
- Cost: $500-1,500 for your situation
- ROI: Typically saves 3-5x their fee in found deductions

**After Year 1:** Once a CPA has set up your structure (S-Corp, retirement accounts, deduction categories), you could transition to software (TurboTax Self-Employed or FreeTaxUSA) for subsequent years if your situation stays stable.

**If you use software:** TurboTax Self-Employed ($120+) or FreeTaxUSA ($0 federal + $15 state) both handle Schedule C, but they won't proactively advise on S-Corp election or tax planning.

## 7. Quarterly Estimated Tax Recommendations

### Who Must Pay Quarterly Estimates?

You must pay if you expect to owe $1,000+ in federal tax after withholding and credits. With 1099 income, this almost certainly applies to you.

### Federal Estimated Tax Due Dates (2026 for Tax Year 2026)

| Quarter | Period | Due Date |
|---------|--------|----------|
| Q1 | Jan 1 - Mar 31 | April 15, 2026 |
| Q2 | Apr 1 - May 31 | June 15, 2026 |
| Q3 | Jun 1 - Aug 31 | September 15, 2026 |
| Q4 | Sep 1 - Dec 31 | January 15, 2027 |

### How Much to Pay

**Safe Harbor Methods (avoid penalties by paying the lesser of):**
1. **100% of prior year tax** — Pay last year's total tax in 4 equal installments
   - If AGI > $150,000: must pay **110%** of prior year tax
2. **90% of current year tax** — Requires estimating current year income

**Recommended approach:** Use the 100%/110% prior year method — it's penalty-proof regardless of how much you earn this year.

### Indiana Estimated Tax

Indiana also requires quarterly estimates. Same due dates. Pay through [INTIME portal](https://intime.dor.in.gov/).

**Formula:** (Expected Indiana income x 3.05%) + (Expected income x county rate) - any withholding = annual estimate / 4

### Practical Steps

1. Calculate total tax from your 2025 return
2. Subtract expected W-2 withholding for 2026
3. Divide the remainder by 4
4. Set up auto-payments via [IRS Direct Pay](https://www.irs.gov/payments/direct-pay) and INTIME
5. Alternatively: Increase W-2 withholding (submit new W-4) to cover 1099 tax — this avoids quarterly paperwork entirely

## 8. Indiana-Specific Considerations

### Indiana State Tax Rate
- **Flat rate: 3.05%** (2025) — applies to all income levels
- Indiana adjusted gross income generally follows federal AGI with some modifications

### County Income Tax
- **Every Indiana county has its own tax rate** (0.0% to 3.38%)
- Determined by your county of residence on January 1
- Common rates: Marion County 2.02%, Hamilton County 1.0%, Allen County 1.48%
- **Action:** Verify your county rate at [IN.gov](https://www.in.gov/dor/tax-rates/county-income-tax-rates/)

### Indiana Tax Credits (often overlooked)

1. **529 College Savings Credit** — 20% credit on contributions up to $7,500/taxpayer
   - With 3 kids, both spouses contributing = up to $3,000 annual state credit
   - **This is the single best Indiana-specific strategy**

2. **Unified Tax Credit for the Elderly** — If applicable for parents you support

3. **Indiana Earned Income Credit** — 10% of federal EITC (if you qualify)

4. **Residential Energy Credit** — Indiana may offer additional state credits beyond federal

5. **Adoption Credit** — If applicable

6. **Neighborhood Assistance Credit** — For contributions to approved organizations

### Indiana-Specific Deductions

- **Renter's Deduction** — $3,000 if renting (not applicable if homeowner)
- **Human Services Tax Deduction** — contributions to qualified organizations
- **Indiana NOL** — Carried forward differently than federal

### Indiana Filing

- **Form IT-40** for full-year residents
- **Due date:** April 15 (same as federal)
- **E-file** through INfreefile.in.gov (free if AGI < $84,000)
- **Payment:** INTIME portal at intime.dor.in.gov
